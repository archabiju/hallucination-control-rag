# Explainable AI (XAI) for Retrieval Systems

Explainable AI, or XAI, is the broad set of techniques for making a machine learning model's decisions understandable to a human, rather than leaving them as an opaque input-to-output mapping. In classical machine learning, XAI usually means explaining a single prediction — for example, which pixels of an image most influenced a classifier's decision, or which input features drove a model's output up or down. In retrieval and generation systems, the same underlying idea is repurposed to explain not a classification, but a retrieval decision and a generation decision: why was this particular document chunk retrieved for this query, and why did the model's answer take the shape it did.

Several explainability signals are naturally available inside a retrieval-augmented pipeline without requiring any extra specialized model. Similarity scores between the query and each retrieved chunk are themselves a form of explanation — they show, numerically, how confident the retrieval step was in each piece of evidence it surfaced. Chunk attribution — labeling exactly which source document and page a piece of retrieved text came from — turns a generated answer into something auditable: a user or reviewer can trace any claim back to its origin. Provenance scoring extends this idea to the whole answer, measuring what fraction of the generated response is actually traceable to retrieved evidence versus content that appears to have no clear source.

A more fine-grained technique is word-level saliency via leave-one-out perturbation: to estimate how much a specific word in a sentence contributes to that sentence's similarity to the retrieved evidence, the word is temporarily removed and the similarity is recomputed. If removing the word causes similarity to drop sharply, that word was carrying a lot of the sentence's evidential weight (it "anchors" the claim to the source); if removing it barely changes similarity, or even increases it, the word was not contributing evidential support and may be embellishment, filler, or an unsupported addition. Repeating this for every word in a sentence produces a saliency heatmap that visually distinguishes the evidence-grounded parts of a sentence from the parts that are not.

The significance of applying XAI this way to a hallucination-detection system is that it moves explainability from an optional add-on into the core detection mechanism itself. Rather than training a separate black-box classifier to predict "hallucinated or not" and then trying to explain that classifier's decision after the fact, the system's own retrieval and attribution signals — similarity, provenance, and saliency — are the features the hallucination decision is built on directly. This means every hallucination verdict the system produces already comes with a built-in, interpretable justification, rather than requiring a second explanation step bolted on afterward.
